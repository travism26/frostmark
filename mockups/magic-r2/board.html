Magic gear, round 2: the same seven sets, redrawn with depth

Round 1 fixed the names, themes and the ladder (Shardweaver → Rimecaller → Glacier Warden, then Hoarfrost Sovereign, Hearthfire Magus, Aurora Shaman and Ruinwarden at the top). This round keeps every one of those and redraws each set the way the ranged board draws its gear: a list of flat-shaded 3D primitives (domes, cylinders, boxes, hoops, fins, crystals) on the barbarian's bone slots, projected face-on and from the 55° play camera, lit by the fixed dusk sun with blue-violet shadows. Every piece still maps one-to-one onto character_gear.gd mesh primitives. No raster art, no asset packs.

"I like these ideas but we have to make sure we give them a better... do another round on the details. Let's add more details to the gear. All of them look kind of circular. There's no depth to them. They're kind of basic. And... Yeah, they don't look that great from the top down." — the captain, on the round-1 magic board

Each card shows round 1 (dashed, as reviewed) beside round 2, then the set at true play size. The ladder strips at the top put all seven at 1:1. The review for this round happens on the GitHub issue, not on a live board.

What round 2 changes on every set

1 · Break the circles

No piece is a lone ellipse from above any more. Hoods get a swept-back peak and a face opening; shoulders are angled plates, feather wedges or shards; robes open at the front over an under-robe and trail a cloak or tail behind, so each figure has a front and a back.

2 · Layer, don't paint

Two or three overlapping pieces per slot: under-robe, open coat, hem band; collar behind the head; upper and lower shoulder plates; a chest plate with a glyph on it. The dusk light draws a lit top face and a shaded front face on every box, so edges read without textures.

3 · Asymmetry

One shoulder carries more than the other, a satchel or lantern hangs on one hip, a feather sits on one side of the circlet, the coat's two panels are different lengths. From above, the figure is no longer a mirror.

4 · Where the camera looks

Crown, shoulders, staff head and the trailing cloak get the detail; legs get one band. Tier markers are height (peak, crest, crown), width (plates, mantle), and count of crystals, in that order, so the ladder reads at 31 px per metre.

5 · Same primitives, same budget

Domes, cylinders, hoops, boxes, thin fins and four-facet crystals: all SphereMesh, CylinderMesh, TorusMesh, BoxMesh and a small prism mesh, flat materials, emission through Props.flat(colour, glow). Top sets add one OmniLight on the staff and a few glowing motes (the sparse particles the captain allowed).

6 · The depth bar

The ranged board is the bar. These figures use the same projection, the same lit/shadow split and the same base barbarian, so a magic set and a ranged set can be judged side by side at the same size.

The ladder at play size

Play camera: pitch 55°, default distance 32 m, vertical FOV 40° on a 1280×720 frame ≈ 31 px per metre, so a 1.8 m figure is about 56 px tall with its crown. Each strip is at that scale; the last is the closest zoom (14 m, ≈ 71 px/m). The round-1 row at the bottom is at the same scale for comparison.
