Magic gear: seven sets, mid to top

One set each at mid, mid-high and high, then four distinct top sets. Every set has a helm, body, legs and staff. Each is drawn twice: a front view to judge the design, and a top-down view at the game camera's size and angle (55° pitch, default zoom), over ring-one and ring-two snow, so you see what will actually read in play. Everything is composed from the same primitive vocabulary character_gear.gd already uses: domes, cylinders, tori, boxes and prisms, flat-shaded, with Props.flat(colour, glow) emission for the crystals.

How to review: annotate any element directly (click a hood, a hem, a staff head). Under each set, pick Keep, Rework or Drop, add a note, and queue it. The final question at the bottom asks which top sets go to tickets and whether the ladder is final. Sibling boards cover melee and ranged.

What was borrowed, and from where

Principles, not designs. Each card names the source and the one thing taken from it.

Old School RuneScape: Virtus robes

The RS3 original was judged "cluttered and out-of-place" at Old School's scale and redrawn for its "simple and bold aesthetics." Principle: at a small on-screen size, fewer and bolder shapes win. wiki

Old School RuneScape: Ancestral and Mystic

Ancestral is a plain robe-and-hat silhouette made prestigious by colour and a recolour kit; Mystic ships in five colours. Principle: colour is a tier signal on its own; a prestige recolour is cheap endgame. Ancestral · Mystic

Path of Exile: Chaos and Order armour post

GGG "darkened the armour overall to increase the contrast", used one glow colour, and enlarged the motif so it was "large and prominent on all pieces", keeping effects restrained. Principle: dark base, one accent, one big repeated motif. forum

Monster Hunter Rise: Malzeno gear interview

Openings in a silhouette "make a weapon look lighter"; crystals sit at the openings; a red line in the grooves reads as energy flowing from them; heroic colours, minimal "evil" for flagship gear. Principle: put the glow where the silhouette opens; keep top gear heroic. interview

Elden Ring: Lusat's glintstone crown, Snow Witch set

Lusat's is "the giant blue glintstone crown" that replaced his skull: the headpiece is the crystal. The Snow Witch set makes cold magic read as cloth and fur, not metal. Principle: a mage's headpiece carries the tier; cold magic is soft materials plus one mineral. crown · Snow Witch

Valheim Eitr-weave; Diablo IV art direction

Valheim's mage set is hood, robe and trousers that trade armour for regen, in a flat low-poly world. Diablo IV's team describes hand-crafted detail and materials that react to light. Principle: magic gear is visibly lighter than melee gear; lit material beats painted detail. Eitr-weave · D4

Rules the sets follow for frostmark

  • No pointed hat. The frost witch enemy already owns that silhouette (monsters_after_witch_strike.jpg); the player must never be mistaken for her. Player mages wear hoods, circlets and crowns.
  • Cold magic, warm world. The game's one warm colour is firelight. The magic palette stays cold (frost, teal, violet) so the fire stays special, with one deliberate exception at top tier.
  • Grander by step. Head height, shoulder width, hem length, crystal count and glow each rise one notch per tier, so the ladder reads from above where legs barely show.
  • Buildable. Each piece lists the primitives it is made of, mounted on the same bone slots the current helm, vest, kilt and greaves use. Glow is the existing emission flag plus, at top tier, one small OmniLight on the staff.
