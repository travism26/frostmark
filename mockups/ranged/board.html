Frostmark — ranged gear sets

Seven ranged sets for the grill in #138: one each at mid mid-high high, then four distinct top sets. Every figure is drawn the way the game draws gear: flat-shaded primitives (spheres, cylinders, boxes, hoops, flat fins) hung on the barbarian's bone slots, lit by the fixed dusk sun with blue-violet shadows. Each set is shown face-on and from the play camera (55° pitch), and again at 1:1 play size so you can judge what actually reads.

How to answer: annotate anything on a figure, or use the verdict box under each set (Pick / Rework / Reject + a note), then the three board-level questions at the bottom. Queue answers, then Send to Agent. When you are happy, mark the board final.

What was borrowed (principles, not designs)

Silhouette first

At the play camera a character is ~45 px tall, so the read is silhouette, then colour blocks, then nothing else. The tier ladder here is a silhouette ladder: round hood → hood with ear points → cap with feather and quiver → crest, wings, crown or trailing cloak that extend past the body.

RocketBrush on shape language ("~70% of a design's impact comes from its silhouette"; test in greyscale, at distance, in motion). Elden Ring's Raptor's set and Black Knife set read at a glance from one feature each: the feather ruff and the hooded cloak.

Value contrast against snow

Frostmark's ground is lavender-blue snow and the shadows are violet. Warm or dark gear pops; pale cool gear sinks. Each set keeps a dark or warm anchor (hood, underside, straps) so the figure stays a shape on the snow. The cool top set is the deliberate exception and is flagged as a risk.

Readability sources agree value beats hue; Monster Hunter's Legiana ("glistens a piercing blue") versus Nargacuga (black fur, pale mask) shows two opposite anchors that both read.

60 / 30 / 10 colour blocking

One body colour, one secondary (straps, pelt, plates), one accent. The accent is the only thing allowed to glow, and only on top sets: tips, hems, eyes, nocks. Never a whole surface, so the GL-compatibility renderer gets one emissive flat material per piece, as the frost staff tip already does.

RuneScape's ranged line (leathers → god-hides → Armadyl/Masori) escalates by trim and crest, not by changing the leather base. Path of Exile players describe "high tier" as trims, added layers and glowing runes, and as being physically bigger.

Ranged has its own shape

Hoods and caps instead of helms; a quiver over the right shoulder; a bow in the left hand; narrower shoulders than melee until the top tier adds wings or a mantle. Legs stay slim (wraps, chaps) so the archer never reads as a knight.

Diablo IV's rogue (hood, layered leathers), Valheim's Fenris (hooded fur, light), Monster Hunter bow sets (cloth and hide with one hard plate).

Warm light in a cold world

Two of the four top sets carry the game's own palette: amber (firelight) and aurora teal (the outer rings' sky). Glow colours are the existing COLOR_FROST and the fire's amber, so the sets look like they belong to this world, not to a shop.

#40 art direction; scenes/ring_look.gd sun (1.0, 0.8, 0.6) and ambient (0.52, 0.47, 0.70).

Each tier visibly grander

Grandeur steps: more pieces that stick out, a second colour, then a glow. Mid has none of those, mid-high has one, high has two, top has all three. The ladder strip below is the test: cover the labels and the order should still be obvious.

Valheim's biome ladder (leather → troll → root/iron → wolf/fenris → padded) reads by material and bulk, not by stat.

The ladder at play size

Play camera: pitch 55°, default distance 32 m, vertical FOV 40° on a 1280×720 frame ≈ 31 px per metre, so a 1.8 m figure is ~45 px tall on screen. Each strip is at that scale. Below it, the same figures at the closest zoom (14 m, ≈ 71 px/m).

The sets

Board-level calls

1. Where the four tier names sit

Assumed for this board: mid = the iron era (ring one), mid-high = the steel era (ring two), high = a new smithable tier past steel, top = named sets that are found, not smithed. Sibling boards (melee, magic) use the same names.

2. Quivers, mantles and cloaks

The mid-high set and above carry a quiver; the top sets add a mantle, wings or a cloak. On this board they are drawn as part of the body piece (no new slot). #138 question 1 asks whether a cape slot should exist.

3. Is this board final?

Final means: the picks and reworks you marked above go into the report as the ranged line, and tickets can be cut from it. Another round means I redraw whatever you annotated and re-present.
