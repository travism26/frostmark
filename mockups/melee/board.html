Melee gear that looks worth carrying home

Seven melee sets drawn in the game's own look: low-poly, flat-shaded, built from the same primitives scenes/character_gear.gd already hangs on bone slots. One set each at mid, mid-high and high, then four distinct top sets. Each is shown from the front and from the 55° top-down camera, and the ladder strip at the top shows all of them at the size they actually have on screen.

Your job on this board: pick, mark up, or reject each set. Click any part of a drawing to annotate it. Each set has a verdict form; queue one answer per set, then press Mark the board final at the bottom when you are done. Ranged and magic sets are on their own boards.

What reads from the camera

At the default zoom the character is about 60 px tall on a 1280 px frame, seen 55° from above. The helm crown and the shoulders are most of the visible pixels; the chest is a sliver, the legs are hidden under the kilt, and the weapon is a short line.

So every tier marker lives on the crown, the shoulders and the weapon's head. Legs and chest detail are for the equipment tab and the close camera only.

Borrowed principles, not designs

The references we looked at and the one rule each gave us. Sources are cited in the report.

Bigger shapes, cut what hurts readabilityDiablo IV's art team sizes armour detail to the isometric camera: "bigger shapes on armor" and anything that hurts readability is cut. Colours must group, silhouettes must stand out.
Blizzard, Diablo IV quarterly update, June 2021 (John Mueller, Nick Chilano)
Trim on a familiar shape makes a familyRuneScape's god-trimmed sets keep the rune silhouette and change only trim colour, a plume and spikes. A ladder reads as one family when the base shape persists and the trim escalates.
OSRS wiki, Bandos armour pages
Colour between the plates; a creature motifMonster Hunter's Malzeno set puts red in the gaps between carapace plates, borrows the monster's own anatomy for the shapes, and cuts openings into the outline so heavy forms read lighter.
Capcom developer interview on the Malzeno gear, Sunbreak
Iconic means removable-testPath of Exile's concept team: an "iconic" design is recognisable from far away, and removing one key component changes how it reads. "Theme-stacking" layers a second idea on a plain base.
Grinding Gear Games forum post on the NZGDC concept-art talk
Lighting over textureValheim's low-res, sparse-polygon look works because composition and lighting carry it. Frostmark has the same bet: flat shading and the dusk light do the work, so one emissive accent goes a long way.
Iron Gate on Valheim's art style; Robin Eyre interview
Glow as a break from heavy metalElden Ring's best-loved sets each own one strong idea: Blaidd's wolf mask and cape, Malenia's gold on red, Cleanrot's glow against dark plate. One idea per set, pushed hard.
Elden Ring community roundups (weak sources, used only for the one-idea rule)
How the ladder escalates

Each rung adds exactly one silhouette element above the rung below, in the order the camera sees them: mid adds a second material (leather and brass on blue steel); mid-high adds a creature motif on the crown and fur on the shoulders; high adds height (a crest and plume) and the first faint glow; top sets each own one bold idea and a colour nobody else uses, and are the only tier allowed a light or particles. Palette stays inside the art direction: cold base metals, blue-violet shadow, and one warm or luminous accent per set.
